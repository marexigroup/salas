Seasonal operation creates scarcity and anticipation. Alumni community = free marketing. Communal meals are the single most effective community-building tool — cheap to run, high social return.

🇪🇸

Nonprofit "pueblo coliving" in a mountain village of 350 people. Nomads live in local houses, cowork in a municipal building with 1 Gbps fibre. The "team offsite" market is adjacent and high-value: a company blocks 8 rooms for a week at €1,500+/person. Worth building a corporate offering alongside individual stays. No kitchen conflict — groups don't mix.

Ubud & Canggu, Bali, Indonesia
🇮🇩
2–6 month stays Coworking separate 3 Bali locations Long-stay focus
$500–900/month
Premium end of Bali coliving. Keeps coworking buildings physically separate from living spaces — a short walk that creates psychological separation between "work mode" and "home." Separating work and living spaces — even if only 100 metres apart — reduces friction and burnout. Nomads who "live at work" leave early. A distinct coworking space (even a designated café or room in the building) is worth designing deliberately.

Hubud
Ubud, Bali, Indonesia (closed 2019)
🇮🇩
Pioneer 2013–2019 Bamboo coworking Culture-first Cautionary tale
The global pioneer that put Bali on the digital nomad map. Iconic bamboo coworking structure in the rice fields. Closed in 2019. Lessons: its identity was inseparably tied to its physical building — when that building aged, so did the brand. It had no housing component and relied entirely on the community it built around daily coworking rituals.
What to learn

Being first in a category creates enormous brand equity — but physical spaces need reinvestment. The community outlived the building (Hubud alumni still meet globally). Your brand must be portable enough to survive beyond the physical space: invest in alumni network from day one.

Cautionary Tale

What happens when you scale before you have community
Warning
Selina
100+ locations, 25 countries — went public 2022
🌍
IPO via SPAC 2022 –96% market cap 2024 Overexpansion No shared kitchen
Raised $300M+, expanded to 100+ locations across LATAM, Europe, and Asia. Marketed heavily as "coliving for digital nomads." Market cap collapsed from peak to $3.28M by mid-2024 — a 96.78% wipeout. Root causes: quality inconsistency between locations, spending on marketing over community, properties not designed for long-term living (no kitchens), and treating coliving as a hotel product rather than a community product.
The lesson for El Primitivo

Stay small and deep rather than wide and thin. 8 rooms done exceptionally well generates better economics, better reviews, and better word-of-mouth than 80 rooms done adequately. Community is the product — the rooms are just where you sleep.

Key Lessons from the Field

What the best projects have in common
01
Community is the product. Rooms are just where you sleep.

Every successful rural coliving — Anceu, Sende, SunDesk, Outpost — has a daily ritual that forces interaction: communal meals, a shared breakfast, a morning coworking session. Without this, you are running an apartment rental. With it, you are running a community that happens to have beds.

Source: Selina failure analysis · SunDesk model · Sende alumni data

02
The "dying village" story is a free media machine.

Rooral was featured in Euronews, BBC, and 15+ publications — unpaid — purely because they quantified their local economic impact (€1,765/nomad/month spent locally). The "we are reversing rural depopulation" angle is exactly what European media is looking for in 2026. Salas fits this narrative perfectly. Document the numbers from month one.

Source: Euronews "Half our villages are dying" · Rooral press coverage

03
Stay length sweet spot: 1 month minimum.

Shorter stays (1–2 weeks, Coconat model) generate higher per-night revenue but create a constant churn of strangers who never form community. The 1-month minimum is where nomads actually relax, integrate, and recommend you to others. It also makes operations simpler: fewer check-ins, more predictable revenue.

Source: Genki coliving guide 2026 · Outpost model analysis

04
Small is a feature, not a limitation.

8–16 people is the magic number across the best-reviewed colivings globally. Everyone knows everyone within 48 hours. Decisions happen fast. The founder is present. Quality is consistent. Nomads specifically choose small colivings to avoid the anonymous hotel feeling. Selina's 50–200 room properties failed partly because of this. Never expand beyond what you can personally quality-control.

Source: SunDesk (16 max) · Sende (~8) · Selina collapse analysis

05
Day passes and walk-in coworking build community for free.

Dojo Bali grew its resident community by selling daily coworking passes first. Walk-in nomads became residents; residents became ambassadors. The Camino Primitivo provides El Primitivo with a natural version of this: pilgrims and nomad day-trippers walking through Salas. A café/coworking space open to walk-ins creates organic social proof and revenue from day one.

Source: Dojo Bali model · Camino Primitivo foot traffic data

06
Heritage and UNESCO status are marketing shorthand.

Kalart leads with "UNESCO Biosphere Reserve." Outpost leads with "Balinese temples and sacred forests." These labels instantly communicate authenticity and scarcity to a nomad who is comparing 15 colivings at once. The Camino Primitivo is a UNESCO World Heritage route — this needs to be the first line in every listing, not buried in an About section.

Source: Kalart positioning · Outpost marketing analysis

Part 2 — The Broader Movement

Beyond Coliving: Intentional Communities at Every Scale

The same underlying hunger — meaningful place, chosen community, escape from the default city — now manifests at scales from pop-up city experiments (200 people, 2 months) all the way to billion-dollar new city projects. El Primitivo sits at the accessible, authentic European end of this spectrum. Understanding the full landscape reveals both the demand and the positioning opportunity.

Global Coliving Networks

Platform plays — network effects, modular housing, membership
Global Platform
NOMAD Nation
12 cities — Raleigh, Budapest, Prague, Lisbon + more
🌐
12 locations Modular housing App + membership Co-ownership model
$25–60/month membership
Global co-living ecosystem of modular, high-quality housing for digital nomads. Members pay $25–60/month for the app and "circuit trips" across locations. Units range from 130 sq ft micro-rooms (ATOM) to 2,150 sq ft full apartments. Revenue from memberships, property floor plan sales ($299–399), and investment in development. Target: remote workers, content creators, serial entrepreneurs aged 25–50.
What to learn

The platform/network play is their moat — switching locations within NOMAD is easy. El Primitivo cannot match network breadth. The counter-positioning: NOMAD is optimized for movement; El Primitivo is optimized for depth. Market to nomads who are tired of bouncing between identical modular units and want to actually be somewhere.

Próspera
Roatán Island, Honduras — ZEDE Special Economic Zone
🇭🇳
1,700+ residents 40+ nationalities 1% effective tax 200+ businesses formed
1% eff. tax vs. standard rates
A startup governance zone on a Honduran island, 20 min from the international airport. Custom legal code, 1% effective income tax (10% applied to 10% of gross income), independent dispute resolution, digital e-governance. 1,700+ residents from 40+ countries, $100M+ invested, 950 active jobs. Legal stability guarantee: taxes cannot change without business consent. Vitalia pop-up city runs inside Próspera annually.
What to learn

1,700 people relocated to Honduras for governance reasons — proving that community-building is more powerful than geography. Próspera's residents are exactly the audience that would visit El Primitivo for a European base: entrepreneurial, internationally mobile, seeking intentional communities. Consider activating in Próspera/Vitalia circles as an early-adopter marketing channel.

NEOM
Tabuk Province, northwest Saudi Arabia
🇸🇦
$500B+ investment 26,500 km² Government-backed The Line + Oxagon
$500B+ development cost
Saudi Arabia's $500B+ new city project: 26,500 km², 468 km of coastline, 41 islands. Multiple districts: The Line (linear city), Oxagon (industrial port), Sindalah (luxury island). 1.5 GW AI data center under construction. Active 24/7 port operations. 4.7M plants planted. 100+ nationalities on-site. Massive scale, top-down government project. Aimed at attracting global talent and industry.
What to learn

NEOM is the ultimate top-down "build it and they will come" experiment. The lesson for El Primitivo is inverse: authenticity and bottom-up community cannot be manufactured at scale. The same nomads who are skeptical of NEOM's engineered environment are the ones who will seek out Salas. "Real" is the product that $500B cannot buy.

Strategic Synthesis

What the full landscape means for El Primitivo
A
The demand is proven at every scale — the affordable European end is wide open.

Praxis raised $500M in commitments. Próspera attracted 1,700 residents to Honduras. Zuzalu spawned 20+ derivative communities. The demand for intentional physical community is enormous and proven. The affordable, authentic, accessible European version of this — a 1-month stay in a medieval Asturian village — does not yet exist at the quality level El Primitivo could deliver.

B
Pop-up city alumni are your first target audience.

Zuzalu and Vitalia attendees spend 2 months in intensive community and then need to be somewhere for the rest of the year. They are already comfortable with intentional communities, already know other nomads, and are already paying premium prices for curated experiences. Activate in Edge City, Zuzalu Telegram, and Vitalia channels before the first guest arrives.

C
"Real" is the product $500B cannot manufacture.

NEOM costs $500B and cannot produce a medieval castle, a 9th-century pilgrimage route, or a cider house that has been open since before the internet. The more capital floods into engineered intentional communities, the more valuable authentic ones become. Salas is not competing with NEOM — it is the opposite of NEOM. Use this explicitly in positioning.

D
Build the community narrative from month one — it compounds.

Afropolitan built its brand before its building. Zuzalu built 20 derivative communities from one 2-month event. Build Cities tracks local economic impact as a differentiator. The common thread: community narrative compounds over time and is impossible to retrofit. Document every resident, every local connection, every euro spent in Salas from the first week. That data becomes the story that gets El Primitivo into Euronews, Nomad List, and the Zuzalu newsletter.
